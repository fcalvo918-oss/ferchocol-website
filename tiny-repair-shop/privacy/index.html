Tiny Repair Shop — Privacy policy

Who we are

Tiny Repair Shop is provided by Ferchocol Software. For privacy questions, parents or guardians can contact hello@ferchocol.com. Updated November 3, 2026.

Information used by the game

This version does not ask for a game account, name, email address, birth date, location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone access. It asks for the current player’s age range (under 13, 13–17, 18 or older), with a Prefer not to say choice. Only that range is stored on the device, in private preferences excluded from game exports and Android backup. No full birth date is collected.

It includes the Google Mobile Ads SDK for adult debug testing, and no separate analytics SDK. Players under 18, players who skip the age question and players with an unknown privacy status receive no ad requests in this version. For adult players, Google User Messaging Platform (UMP) 4.0 checks applicable privacy requirements and displays configured messages when required. UMP can process device/app information, network information such as IP address, and privacy choices. The app refreshes adult privacy status each launch and uses Google’s current can-request-ads result; errors keep ads off. Required privacy options can be reopened from Settings. Google privacy requests use the app’s AdMob application ID; live ad units remain disabled. Google demo ads are off by default and can only be enabled in a debug build for an adult test session. The signed internal-testing release includes the ads SDK but cannot enable or display these demo ads. Demo ads are available only in Android Studio debug builds. Before an adult enables testing, the game does not initialize the ads SDK or request ads; Android may load the SDK initialization provider. Advertising ID and Android AdServices permissions are removed from this build. During an enabled test session, Google may process device and network information, IP address, diagnostic information and ad interactions to deliver and measure demo ads. Google’s SDK may also handle app/device identifiers, such as app set ID. Removing advertising-ID permission does not remove every identifier. These are test ads, not revenue-generating ads. Only adult ad requests can pass the native privacy check. General-audience content limits and disabled personalization are configured before ad SDK initialization. Changing player/privacy choices invalidates previously queued ads. The SDK initialization provider may still load at startup, as noted above.

A remembered purchase prevents automatic interstitial ad loads and displays. Adult testers, including purchased players, may separately choose to load and watch a Google rewarded demo ad for one hint. Loading and watching each require a deliberate action. The app uses Google’s reward callback to grant a single hint on the same unfinished repair; closing early without that callback or a failed request grants no hint. It does not automatically load another rewarded ad after dismissal. Ending the automatic-ad test session stops further automatic requests and discards its queued ad. Rewarded ads are a separate, explicit choice. Google may already have processed earlier requests. See Google’s privacy policy at https://policies.google.com/privacy.

When you open Remove Ads in Settings, the Google Play Billing Library asks Google Play for product availability and the localized price using the Play account on your device. In this internal test build, adult license testers can initiate a Google Play purchase and restore purchases. From a non-adult or unspecified player setting, an adult-confirmation challenge and explicit adult acknowledgment are required before starting a new purchase. This is a local adult-action safeguard, not identity verification or verified parental consent. It does not change the player’s age setting. Restoring an existing purchase remains available without starting a new charge. Google Play processes the transaction using the Play account and payment method selected in its payment sheet. Use a test payment method when testing. Ferchocol Software does not receive your card details. The app checks purchase ownership on launch, on returning to the app, and when using Restore purchases. Signed purchase information is verified and stored in private app preferences to remember access. The purchase record is excluded from exported game backups and Android backup; on a new installation, use Google Play to restore it.

On Android, the app checks the system’s validated internet connection before starting a repair for free players. A locally verified purchase enables offline repairs. One last unfinished repair started online can be resumed or finished offline; its repair identifier is stored in separate private preferences, excluded from manual and Android backups. This record is not a purchase. An offline or failed Play check preserves the remembered verified purchase; restoring a purchase on a fresh installation requires internet. Network validation uses Android’s system status rather than a new game-server request. The game does not send gameplay information to Ferchocol Software servers.

Progress on your device

The game stores puzzle positions, move and hint counts, completed repairs, stars, daily repair dates and rewards, sound preferences, tutorial status and chosen decorations in local app storage. This information makes it possible to resume play and remember your choices. It is not encrypted by the game itself.

Manual backup files

You can choose to export a game backup and select a file to restore. These files contain game progress and preferences, are not encrypted by the game, and remain wherever you choose to save them, including any cloud storage provider you select. Deleting the app does not delete exported backups. Manage or delete those files through your file provider. Restoring replaces local progress after confirmation and keeps one local copy of the previous save for recovery.

Device backup

Android backup is enabled for this app. Depending on your device and backup settings, Android may back up and restore app data through your device backup provider. Ferchocol Software does not operate that backup service and cannot guarantee that progress will be backed up or restored.

Retention and deletion

Progress stays in local app storage until the app data is cleared, the app is removed, or the device removes it. The game keeps the most recent 14 daily puzzle states and daily results, along with accumulated daily bonus stars and streak records. Clearing app storage removes local progress and preferences. Device backups may be retained separately; manage those through your device or backup provider.

Children and families

The game is intended for children and adults. This version has no game accounts, chat or public profiles. Purchase testing and optional debug ad testing are intended for adult testers only. Children should not enable debug ad testing. The local adult-confirmation step, intended audience, SDK behavior and consent configuration still need review before a public monetized release. No claim of verified parental consent or full Families compliance is made. The game does not ask children to enter personal information. A parent or guardian should handle any support correspondence and avoid sending unnecessary personal information about a child.

Contacting support

If you choose to email hello@ferchocol.com, your email provider and our email service handle that message. We receive your email address and anything you include, and use it to respond to your inquiry. This correspondence is separate from gameplay. You can ask us to delete support correspondence; we may retain information needed to meet legal obligations or resolve an ongoing issue.

Sharing and changes

We do not sell gameplay information or send puzzle progress to advertisers. Google handles demo ad requests during an enabled adult debug test session as described above. Android backup and voluntarily sent support email are described above. Before a public release introduces live advertising or other new data handling, we will update this policy and provide any required notices and choices before enabling those features.
